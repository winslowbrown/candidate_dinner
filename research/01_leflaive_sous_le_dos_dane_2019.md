# Domaine Leflaive — Meursault 1er Cru "Sous le Dos d'Âne" 2019

*Research dossier for the Claud candidate dinner, 26 Oct 2026. Prepared 5 Sept 2026. Confidence tags: [HIGH] = primary source or multiple concordant sources; [MED] = single credible source or minor inconsistency; [LOW] = single secondary source, unverified, or inferred.*

**Headline facts to have at your fingertips**

- A Blagny-sector climat that must be sold as *Meursault* 1er Cru when white (and as *Blagny* 1er Cru when red). Leflaive's only Meursault, and its only holding outside Puligny in the Côte de Beaune. [HIGH]
- Leflaive's parcel was **Pinot Noir until 2002** (Domaine Leflaive bottled a *Blagny 1er Cru Sous le Dos d'Âne rouge* through the 2001 vintage), then replanted to Chardonnay in 2004; another section was planted in 1995. [HIGH]
- The parcel sits "just above Pierre Morey's Meursault Perrières" (William Kelley) — Pierre Morey being Leflaive's own régisseur from 1988 to July 2008. [HIGH]
- Élevage here is different from the rest of the Leflaive 1ers: **large-format barrels, mainly 400 litres** (Bill Nanson, 2018 and 2019 cellar visits). [HIGH]
- 2019 at Leflaive: 5 frost nights, coulure/millerandage, two heatwaves, ~30% volume loss, harvest from 11 September, pH ~3.1 "like a cold year" despite ~14%+ alcohol. Nanson: in this parcel "some vines had their grapes completely grilled" — and yet he called the 2019 the best Sous le Dos d'Âne in years: "this wine is BACK!" [HIGH]
- Closure: DIAM (reported as Diam 30) on everything since the 2014 vintage, after the domaine's catastrophic 2006–2008 premox run. The 2019 is therefore in the "post-premox" era. [HIGH]
- Scores: Burghound 90–93 (Jun 2021); Wine Advocate/Kelley 92 (Jan 2022, "Best after 2025"); Vinous/Neal Martin 92 (Dec 2021); Wine Spectator 93. [HIGH]

## 1. The vineyard / climat

### 1.1 Location, size, exposure, altitude

- **Commune / appellation.** Sous le Dos d'Âne is one of the four Blagny-hillside climats that lie within the commune of Meursault (with La Jeunelotte, La Pièce sous le Bois and Sous Blagny); the other three Blagny 1er crus (Hameau de Blagny, La Garenne/Sur la Garenne, Sous le Puits) lie in Puligny-Montrachet. Reds from any of these seven are *Blagny 1er Cru*; whites take the commune name — *Meursault 1er Cru* (or the historic "Meursault-Blagny") for the Meursault ones, *Puligny-Montrachet 1er Cru* for the Puligny ones. [HIGH — Wikipedia Blagny AOC; BIVB bourgogne-wines.com Blagny page; Louis Latour tech sheet]
  - Note one inconsistency: the French reference site abcduvin.com lists the climat's commune as "Puligny-Montrachet" while simultaneously saying its whites are labelled Meursault. Every other source (INAO climat lists via Wikipedia/BIVB, Louis Latour, Leflaive, Wilson Daniels) puts it in Meursault. Treat abcduvin's commune field as an error. [HIGH]
- **Surface.** 5.03 ha for the whole climat (abcduvin, citing the 21 May 1970 classification). Louis Latour says "about 5 hectares". [HIGH] The entire lieu-dit is classified 1er cru ("La totalité du lieu-dit est classée" — abcduvin). [MED]
- **Position on the slope.** abcduvin: "Il se situe sous le climat La Pièce sous le Bois. Le vignoble est disposé dans la pente d'une butte naturelle nommée le Dos d'âne face au climat de Meursault, Les Perrières" — i.e. it lies *below* La Pièce sous le Bois and *faces/adjoins* Meursault Les Perrières. [HIGH] Olivier Leflaive's tech material (via Frederick Wildman, summarised) places its parcel "adjacent to Meursault Perrières Dessus on Blagny territory". [MED — the Wildman page has since been taken down; only a summarised fetch survives]
- **Altitude & exposure.** Sources disagree slightly:
  - Wilson Daniels 2024 fact sheet (Leflaive's importer): **300 m, south-east exposure**. [HIGH for Leflaive's parcel]
  - Louis Latour (for its blended Meursault-Blagny, per The Burgundy Briefing): 300–360 m, east-facing, "Bathonian limestone" with "thinnish top soil". [MED]
  - BIVB for the Blagny hillside as a whole: "At altitudes of 340 to 400 metres, this hillslope lies on marls overlain by limestone scree. Lower down are clay-limestone soils." [HIGH for Blagny generally; SDA being the lowest of the Blagny climats on the Meursault side, the ~300–340 m figures for the parcel are consistent with this]
- **Soil.** Leflaive: "clay-limestone soil" / "sol argilo-calcaire" (leflaive.fr); "calcareous clay" (Wilson Daniels). abcduvin: clay marking the upper layers over a calcareous base ("un sol marqué par l'argile sur un sol calcaire"). Louis Latour: "marly limestone". Olivier Leflaive (via Wildman, summarised): "a fat, marly and stony soil, which gives the flint mineral flavor"; Berry Bros on Olivier Leflaive's wine: "the red soil adds some mid-palate weight" and "the higher-altitude location in Blagny always ensures this wine retains freshness". [HIGH on the clay-over-limestone picture; MED on the "red soil" detail]

### 1.2 Name etymology

"Dos d'âne" = "donkey's back", the standard French term for a hump-backed ridge or speed-bump. The climat is "sous" (below) a natural hillock of that name. Wilson Daniels/Leflaive: "The name comes from the location of the climat, just below a small mound to the west, going up the hill"; Leflaive's fact sheet glosses it as "Below the ridgeline sloping on each side". abcduvin: "une butte naturelle nommée le Dos d'âne". [HIGH]

### 1.3 History & classification

- Blagny hamlet: worked from the 12th century by the Cistercian monks of Maizières, "known as the 'Montrachet fathers'" (BIVB). [HIGH]
- The Château de Blagny estate was acquired by the de Montlivaut/Montlivault family in 1811; for much of the late 20th century it was run by a widowed countess who sold grapes in bulk rather than bottling; the estate has since split between the Martelet (de Cherisey) line — Domaine Chapelle de Blagny, ~9 ha — and the de Bréchard line — Domaine de Blagny, ~7 ha (Jasper Morris, Inside Burgundy overview). The Burgundy Briefing: "A large part of the vineyard has belonged for the past 200 years to Montlivault, an old aristocratic family," from whom Louis Latour has bought "for a very long time". [HIGH]
- 1860: the Comité d'agriculture de l'arrondissement de Beaune ranked "Sous Le Dos d'Ane" **1ère classe** (as it did La Jeunellotte, La Pièce sous le Bois, Sous Blagny, Perrières, Charmes, Genevrières…; Gouttes d'Or was only 2ème classe). [HIGH — abcduvin]
- 31 July 1937: Meursault AOC decree; BIVB dates the Blagny AOC grant to 1937 as well (Wikipedia says the *current* Blagny AOC dates from 1970 — both can be right: 1937 recognition, 1970 decree). [MED]
- 13 June 1939: Comité national des origines selects the Meursault climats entitled to "premier cru"; 14 October 1943 decree authorises the mention (min. 196 g/L sugar, 11.5% for whites, max 32 hl/ha). 21/25 May 1970: decree fixing the list of 1er cru climats (abcduvin gives 21 May for the Blagny SDA classification and 25 May for the Meursault list). [HIGH]
- The Meursault no-grand-cru point: Meursault Perrières was the climat singled out by Camille Rodier (1920) as exceptional among Meursault's crus (monocepage.com summary), and the 1936–43 INAO process left Meursault with no grand cru — a live topic at any Tastevin table. Sous le Dos d'Âne sits immediately above Perrières; nobody has ever argued it for grand cru status, but its 1860 1ère classe ranking equals Perrières'. [MED — the Rodier/Perrières point is from a secondary site; the 1860 equivalence is from abcduvin]

### 1.4 Neighbours and how the site differs

- **Below/adjacent:** Meursault Les Perrières (Perrières Dessus on the Blagny side). Domaine Pierre Morey's 0.52 ha Perrières parcel, in "Perrières dessous" per Morey's own site (elsewhere summarised as at the upper slope near Sous le Dos d'Âne), "in the South of Meursault, just next to Puligny-Montrachet… a small, very stony 'clos'… because of its proximity to an old quarry." Kelley explicitly locates Leflaive's SDA "just above Pierre Morey's Meursault Perrières". [HIGH]
- **Above:** La Pièce sous le Bois (Blagny/Meursault 1er cru), then the Bois de Blagny. [HIGH]
- **Along the hill towards Puligny:** the other Blagny climats, then Puligny's Hameau de Blagny, Sous le Puits, La Garenne, and below them Champ Gain / Folatières. Leflaive itself describes the parcel as "on the edge of the path between Puligny and Meursault", expressing both neighbouring terroirs (leflaive.fr). [HIGH]
- **Stylistic difference from Perrières:** higher, cooler, more clay and marl (versus Perrières' quarry-stone), hence typically more linear, less powerful, more "Puligny-like" — Neal Martin on the 2019: "a very Puligny-like bouquet"; K&L on Olivier Leflaive's version: "A Meursault that's almost a Puligny, both in terms of geography and style." Louis Latour frames the Blagny reds as "fruity, full-bodied and tannic"; for whites, the Burgundy Briefing's Latour vertical notes the site historically "disappeared into a 1er Cru Meursault blend" before the mid-1970s. [MED — critics' impressions]

## 2. Leflaive's plot within the vineyard

### 2.1 Size — a primary-source conflict

- Wilson Daniels (US importer) fact sheets for 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024, and its web page: "Sous le Dos d'Âne – Below the ridgeline sloping on each side, **1ha 62a (4 acres)**. 1 parcel in Meursault, planted in 1995 and 2004." [HIGH as a quotation]
- leflaive.fr (2024 vintage page, EN and FR): "Surface area: **1ha18a (2.91 acres)**", "Plantings from 1995 and 2004". [HIGH as a quotation]
- Reconciliation attempts: (a) 1.18 ha may be the currently *producing* Chardonnay area vs 1.62 ha total parcel (a section could be in replanting/fallow — Leflaive has been replanting c. 3 ha in Puligny, per leflaive.fr); (b) one of the two is a typo. Older third-party summaries of the domaine's holdings do not itemise SDA. **I trust 1.62 ha for the parcel** because it appears consistently across four annual importer fact sheets that Leflaive supplies, and 1.18 ha as a possible current-production figure. [MED]

### 2.2 Position, vines, history

- One single parcel, in Meursault, "just above Pierre Morey's Meursault Perrières" (Kelley, Wine Advocate, Jan 2022). [HIGH]
- Plantings: **1995 and 2004** (leflaive.fr; Wilson Daniels; Nanson 2017/2018/2019 reports all repeat "from vines planted in 2004 and 1995"). [HIGH]
- The red-to-white conversion: "Pinot Noir was planted here up until 2002 and the wine was then called Blagny Premier Cru instead of Meursault" (Wilson Daniels/Leflaive fact sheet). Domaine Leflaive's *Blagny 1er Cru Sous le Dos d'Âne rouge* is documented for 1999 (wine.com listing, confirmed as Pinot Noir) and 2001; a CellarTracker note on the 2001 (user "Eric", 26 Oct 2007) records: "Apparently the last Rouge vintage of this wine. The Pinot Noir was subsequently ripped up and planted to Chardonnay." A white SDA existed already from 2002 (a 2002 Meursault 1er Cru Sous le Dos d'Âne appears in the Oxidized-Burgundies wiki, tasted Oct 2011 "No sign of oxidation"), which fits the 1995 Chardonnay planting having come on stream while the older Pinot section was still red. The 2004 planting is therefore the replant of the grubbed-up Pinot section (2002 pull, one fallow season, 2004 plant). [HIGH on the sequence; MED on the exact 2002 vs 2001 last-red year — WD says "until 2002", the CT drinker says 2001 was the last bottled red]
- **On the brief's claim of a c. 2004 acquisition via Château de Blagny / Comtesse de Montlivault: NOT VERIFIED and, on the evidence, unlikely as stated.** Domaine Leflaive was already bottling this climat under its own label in the 1990s and planted Chardonnay in it in 1995, so it controlled the parcel at least a decade before 2004. What *did* happen in 2004 was (i) the replanting of the ex-Pinot section and (ii) the domaine's first Mâconnais purchase (9 ha, leflaive.fr) — either could be the origin of the "2004" memory. I found no source linking Leflaive's SDA to the Montlivault/Château de Blagny estate; the documented Montlivault relationships are with Louis Latour (long-term purchase) and with the Martelet/de Bréchard heirs. Whether Leflaive owns the parcel outright or holds it en fermage from a Blagny landowner: **NOT FOUND**. When it entered the Leflaive portfolio (Joseph's 1905–1930 purchases? Vincent/Jo era?): **NOT FOUND**. [LOW for any acquisition date]
- Rootstock / clonal vs massal selection for the 1995 and 2004 plantings: **NOT FOUND**.
- Density: Meursault 1er cru AOC minimum is 9,000 vines/ha (abcduvin decree summary); Leflaive's actual density NOT FOUND.

### 2.3 Farming

- Organic certification: **Ecocert 1992**; biodynamic: **Biodyvin 1998** (Wilson Daniels 2024 fact sheet). [HIGH] Leflaive's own site says Anne-Claude "enlisted the Domaine to Biodynamy between 1992–1996"; Wikipedia/BBR/Corney & Barrow say full conversion 1997; Wine Spectator's obituary says organic from 1990 and "by 1998, all the vineyards had converted". These are all consistent with: trials from 1990, progressive conversion 1992–96, all-biodynamic 1997, certified 1998. [HIGH]
- Horse ploughing: used "on grand crus and some premiers crus plots (Les Pucelles)" (leflaive.fr). Whether SDA is horse-ploughed: **NOT FOUND**. [MED]
- Pruning: since c. 2022 a two-stage regime advised by Simonit & Sirch — winter pruning Dec–Feb, then a late-March final cut to delay budbreak (Leflaive News #13, Dec 2022, Pierre Vincent). Post-dates the 2019. [HIGH]
- Frost defence: candles, straw fires, wind turbines and covers in 2019 (Leflaive's 2019 vintage text); by 2022 candles were dropped "due to pollution" in favour of late pruning and wind machines (News #13). [HIGH]
- Yields: green harvesting is "rare in Puligny, even rarer at Leflaive" — trialled 2017, done substantially in 2018 (Pierre Vincent & Brice to Nanson, Oct 2019). Not needed in 2019 (short crop). [HIGH]
- The 1995/2004 vines were 24 and 15 years old in 2019 — young by Leflaive standards. [HIGH]

## 3. Winemaking for this cuvée — and 2019 specifically

### 3.1 The current (Pierre Vincent-era) protocol for Sous le Dos d'Âne

From Leflaive's own site and the Wilson Daniels fact sheets (2021–24), plus Nanson's cellar notes:

- **Harvest:** manual, with sorting; date set "through parcel-by-parcel ripeness monitoring" (leflaive.fr). [HIGH]
- **Pressing:** "Long, gentle pneumatic pressing, decanting over 24 hours, then racking and running into cask of the must" (WD). A new press arrived for the 2018 harvest — Pierre Vincent: "we have a new press, which helps us to be even more reactive… it allows extra time for pressing and more precision — 'it's a detail change'" (Nanson, Oct 2018). Leflaive's "The Work of Time" page: "Slow pressing, light use of sulphur to preserve the must, settling at low temperature." [HIGH]
- **Fermentation:** alcoholic *and* malolactic in oak. Yeasts: Leflaive's site speaks only of letting "nature express itself"; the SommSelect profile says "indigenous yeast fermentation in oak casks". [MED on indigenous yeast — importer/retailer sources only]
- **Barrels:** "Alcoholic fermentation in oak casks, **22% new (maximum 1/3 Vosges, minimum 2/3 Allier)**" (WD, every year 2021–24). Nanson, 2018 and 2019 visits: "all the 1ers have 20% new oak, though here the barrels are large-format – **mainly 400 litres**." Cooper: **NOT FOUND** (no cooperage named in any source for Domaine Leflaive; Pierre Vincent's own domaine uses Chassin, Tonnellerie de Mercurey and Berthomieu for reds — not applicable here). Toast: NOT FOUND. [HIGH on 20–22% new and 400-L format; the 20 vs 22% gap is rounding]
- **Bâtonnage:** Pierre Vincent (Oct 2018): "We just started some bâtonnage – usually done until the malo starts." I.e. lees stirring only during/around alcoholic fermentation up to the onset of malo — far less than the Morey era (below). [HIGH]
- **Élevage:** "12 months in barrels on lees then 10 months in stainless steel tank on fine lees" (leflaive.fr, 2024 page) vs "After 12 months in cask, the wine is aged 6 months in tank where it is prepared for bottling" (WD). Leflaive's overview page: "one year in oak barrels then six months in stainless steel vats… eighteen months in the wine storehouse and in the cellar until bottling, two winters". So 12 + 6–10 months depending on year/vintage timing. Puligny village lots "complete their first year of élevage separated – they are only assembled for the second part" (Nanson) — SDA is a single parcel so no assemblage question. [HIGH]
- **Fining/filtration:** "Optional fining and light filtration if necessary" (leflaive.fr) / "Fining and very light filtering if necessary" (WD). [HIGH]
- **Bottling & closure:** DIAM technical cork on the entire range from the 2014 vintage (Jamie Goode, 31 Oct 2016, via Adam Brett-Smith of Corney & Barrow; Nanson repeats "DIAM seals from the 2014 vintage" every year; Corney & Barrow producer page: "Technical Diam corks have been used since 2014"). Grade: **Diam 30** per the Oxidized-Burgundies wiki (Don Cornwell) and Winehog (2017); Neal Martin's Vinous note on the 2019 is reported by K&L as remarking it was "closed with Diam 30". [HIGH on DIAM; MED-HIGH on Diam 30]
- **Alcohol on label:** 12.5% (WD 2024 sheet, generic), 13.5% (2021 sheet). 2019 label ABV: NOT FOUND directly; Jasper Morris reports the 2019 range at 14–14.5%, Pierre Vincent "13.5° for our Mâcons but up to 1 degree more in the Pulignys". [MED]
- **Style:** reductive-leaning. Winehog (2017) describes "the reductive style of Domaine Leflaive"; Kelley on the 2019: "a touch reductive out of the gates"; Martin: "a small reduction at play". Contrast this with the more oxidative/"bâtonnage-heavy" Meursault school — the point to make at the table is that this is a Puligny house making Meursault in the Puligny idiom. [HIGH]

### 3.2 The 2019 timeline for this wine

- Harvest at Leflaive began **10 or 11 September 2019** (Pierre Vincent: "We started our harvesting 11 September and this was a hot period too – the grapes gained 1 degree in 3 days"; Jasper Morris: "Picking began on 10th September"; Leflaive's own vintage page: "the harvest began on 11th September"). Exact picking day for the SDA parcel: NOT FOUND; Folatières is usually first, SDA at ~300 m and SE-facing would not be among the earliest. [HIGH on the start; LOW on the parcel day]
- Yields: Jasper Morris: "respectable yields of 38 hl/ha"; Pierre Vincent: "a reduction of 30% in volume". [HIGH]
- Élevage: barrel through autumn 2020, then tank. The domaine, for Covid reasons, moved its press tasting to spring 2021 "as the wines are closer to bottling". When Nanson tasted on 29 March 2021 the Mâcons had been bottled three weeks earlier, "the bottling of the Bourgogne would start the following week, culminating with the grand crus in June" — so the **SDA was bottled roughly April–May 2021**, ~19–20 months after harvest, under DIAM. [MED — inferred from the bottling sequence Nanson describes]
- Vintage-specific handling of this parcel: Nanson, from the 2019 visit — "This suffered a lot from the sun in 2019, some vines had their grapes completely grilled." I.e. sunburnt bunches on this SE-facing, young-vine, clay-marl site in the late-June/late-July heatwaves. No chaptalisation or acidification mentioned (none was needed in 2017 "thanks to the biodynamics", and 2019 had 14%+ potential). [HIGH]

### 3.3 Historical regime (Pierre Morey era, 1988–2008) — for context on premox

From Don Cornwell's Oxidized-Burgundies wiki (compiled from domaine statements and Tanzer interviews):

> "The wines, except for Bourgogne, are fermented in cask (20% new for premier cru, 25% for grand crus except Montrachet, which is 100%). Batonnage has been reduced over the years, from initially twice a day during the primary alcoholic fermentation to now no more frequently than twice a week, with the batonnage stopped in December of the vintage year. However the wines are kept on the lees until they are racked just before the next vintage. After that racking, the wines (except for Montrachet) are moved into 10-barrel stainless steel tanks for another 6 to 8 months. The wines are fined with casein and/or bentonite and bottled by gravity usually in April or May, 18 to 20 months after the vintage. Leflaive generally membrane filters the bottom of each vat (about 8 to 10% of the juice) before bottling. Leflaive's free SO2 target level in the finished wine is 25-28 ppm."

Vintage-specific: 1999 bâtonnage "one to two times a week from October to December"; 2002 "I did less bâtonnage in '02… I didn't want the wines to become top heavy" (stirring stopped Dec 2002 though most malos finished April 2003); 2003 average post-fermentation pH 3.57; 2004 "I stirred the lees for a bit longer than usual to help fatten the mid-palates", pH 3.16. [MED-HIGH — secondary compilation but quoting the domaine]

The Vincent-era changes (bâtonnage only to malo onset, no movement of unfinished wine between buildings, new press, DIAM, renovated cellars, new cuverie 2022) are the domaine's answer to that history. Pierre Vincent, speaking in 2025 about his own domaine but plainly reflecting his Leflaive practice: "I like turbidity. I am a partisan for a high level of lees"; six months in stainless after barrel is "essential for pure, clean flavors and harmony on the palate"; "Meursault foie gras is not my style" (World of Fine Wine, Raymond Blake, May 2025). [HIGH as quotes; MED as applied to Leflaive]

### 3.4 Premox at Leflaive — timeline

- Pre-2005: "For a decade, Domaine Leflaive was one of the very small group of producers who had virtually no premox problems" (Cornwell). Jamie Goode: Leflaive was "relatively unscathed" through the 1996-onward industry-wide wave. [HIGH]
- 2005 onward, catastrophically 2006–2008: "beginning with the 2006 vintage, the Leflaive wines have had HUGE oxidation problems with a large percentage of the bottles oxidized… Domaine Leflaive went from one of the six most reliable white burgundies you could buy – to one of the worst performers… Leflaive now acknowledges premox problems with the 2006, 2007 and 2008 vintages" (Cornwell). Goode: "Leflaive reportedly suffered very badly with PremOx from the 2006 vintage onwards." Raymond Blake (WoFW 2025): "far too many bottles were corrupted by premature oxidation, a malaise that Leflaive showed little inclination to address." Tim Atkin (2015): "some of the domaine's wines from the late 1990s and early 2000s did not age well… thanks to better corks, higher levels of sulphur dioxide at bottling and less bâtonnage" the problem has receded. [HIGH]
- Sous le Dos d'Âne specifically in the wiki: 2002 fine (2011); 2005 mixed (fresh May 2012 and Aug 2013, but two oxidised bottles Aug 2013 and Apr 2016); 2006 fine (Apr 2011, "Fat and blowsy"); 2008 one sound (Sep 2014), one oxidised (Jul 2016). [HIGH as reported]
- Nanson (Oct 2018): "It's clear that the domaine was not at its peak since about 2002, their beautifully precocious wines tiring far too quickly, but Brice, at the helm of the domain since 2013 has made some astute decisions; first moving to DIAM closures to put a brake on the wines evolving too quickly, then, perhaps even more astutely hiring Pierre Vincent… Pierre has never openly been a proponent of DIAM." [HIGH]
- The recorking story: Cornwell's wiki (contributor Rayner Cheung) reports rumours since ~2020 that the domaine re-tasted its library, topped up/recorked sound bottles, sealed them with wax or wax/composite capsules and re-released them at a premium; he cites a 2004 Bienvenues seen in Hong Kong in March 2021 at ~US$800. SommSelect refers to "a recorking program [that] addressed pre-2014 cellar stock". **Treat as [LOW-MED]**: reported second-hand, not confirmed by the domaine in any source I found. Worth knowing if a waxed, ex-domaine older Leflaive appears at auction.
- Wax on current releases: NOT FOUND — current bottles are DIAM under a standard capsule.

## 4. The 2019 vintage — at Leflaive and in this wine

### 4.1 Season narrative (Leflaive's own words, "The Alchemy of Vintages", leflaive.fr)

> "After a particularly short winter, the months of February and March are almost spring-like, dry, with even a heat record for the season on 27th February… the weather returned to seasonal cold days from April 5 with a first morning frost followed by four other episodes lasting until 7th May. The lesson of the late frosts of the 2016 and 2017 vintages has been well learned: this time the vignerons have collectively fought against frost by burning candles, burning straw, using wind turbines and laying covers on the vines… straw burning, however spectacular, has not proven effective. The summer of 2019 was one of the hottest to date with two heat waves: one at the end of June and the other at the end of July. It exceeded 40°C in some places and signs of burning appeared on some bunches. Fortunately, the month of August was cooler with rain on the 10th and 12th August… The cool weather early September warmed up and the harvest began on 11th September." [HIGH]

### 4.2 Pierre Vincent and Brice de La Morandière on 2019

To Nanson, 7 Oct 2019 (just after harvest): "Not a crazy vintage despite the high level of sugar, because we have fantastic acidity – we're very happy with the quality; ripe, yellow and of course mature. The one problem is a reduction of 30% in volume – like 2017 was less than in 2014. I think it a more even vintage than 2018. We had 5 frosty nights, oïdium pressure followed by the June heatwave and the July heatwave and hot harvesting conditions. Spring and flowering were far from easy, the flowering took longer than we would like, we ended up with tiny berries, many without pips but after pressing it wasn't the catastrophe for volume that we feared. A good year – plus, probably." [HIGH]

To Nanson, 29 Mar 2021: "5 nights of frost for example, all at the bottom of the slopes, and the weather was also poor for flowering – though good for quality – bringing coulure and millerandes… We started our harvesting 11 September and this was a hot period too – the grapes gained 1 degree in 3 days; we have 13.5° for our Mâcons but up to 1 degree more in the Pulignys but with that really good acidity – pH 3.1 like a cold year – despite the alcohol of a hot year. Sugar and acidity – I think they will live long." [HIGH]

Jasper Morris (visit May 2022): "Picking began on 10th September with respectable yields of 38 hl/ha, degrees between 14 and 14.5% with good acidity… The 2019s are a little richer in alcohol and not quite so fresh and precise as 2020, but are nonetheless high class wines." [HIGH]

Nanson's verdict on the range (Mar 2021): "As far as the Puligny domaine is concerned, that's about as great a set of wines as I can recollect from here. Chapeau!" [HIGH]

Note the frost was "all at the bottom of the slopes" — SDA at ~300 m on the upper hill was not a frost site; its 2019 problem was sun, not cold. [HIGH]

## 5. Stories, people, connections

### 5.1 Anne-Claude Leflaive (17 Jan 1956 – 5 Apr 2015)

- Born in Paris; did harvests in Puligny from her late teens; DTO (oenology technician diploma) at Université de Bourgogne from 1986; joined the domaine full-time 1990 as co-gérante with cousin Olivier; sole director from 1994 after her father Vincent's death (1993). [HIGH — Atkin; Wine Spectator; cavesa.ch]
- The biodynamic conversion was a response to a slump: Atkin — "She did so at a tricky moment, after justified criticism of the wines the domaine produced in the late 1980s and the sense that it had lost its way. Her solution was radical." In 1989 she attended Claude Bourguignon's soil-microbiology seminar (his warning: "Si vous continuez comme cela avec la viticulture chimique vos sols ne contiendront bientôt pas plus de vie que ceux du Sahara") and got François Bouchet's contact from Aubert de Villaine (cavesa.ch). Trials on Clavoillon comparing chemical, organic and biodynamic plots; Bourguignon measured microbial life. The clincher: a blind tasting of two 1996 Clavoillons, organic vs biodynamic, for journalists and importers — "everyone preferred the biodynamically grown one, pronouncing it fresher and more alive. I can still remember the joy on her face" (Atkin). Full conversion 1997; Biodyvin certification 1998. [HIGH]
- Quotes: "We looked at the three types of viticulture, and there was a clear difference in what they produced." / "If you smell the soil in a biodynamic vineyard, it's rich, it's wonderful, it's full of life. When people call me a madwoman for believing in biodynamics, it's because they haven't experienced this." / On a herbicided neighbouring parcel: "It's like a lump of concrete. These people are criminals. They are killing their soils with chemicals." / IMW Winemakers' Winemaker acceptance (2014): "the most important thing for me is to have good grapes and work with respect in the vineyards." [HIGH — Atkin]
- Atkin's premox irony: "it was ironic that Anne-Claude felt the biodynamic Clavoillon also faded less rapidly once opened." And the management detail that matters for the premox story: she "left day to day cellar decisions to her very capable régisseur, Eric Rémy." [HIGH]
- Awards: Decanter Man (Woman) of the Year 2006 ("the world's top white winemaker"); IMW Winemakers' Winemaker 2014. [HIGH]
- Other ventures: Mâconnais purchases from 2004 (Verzé), 20.5 ha by 2015; Clau de Nell in Anjou with husband Christian Jacques (2008); co-founded the École du Vin et des Terroirs at the domaine (2008) with Aubert de Villaine among the founders; active in Nicolas Joly's Renaissance des Appellations. Survived by three daughters (Marine, Charlotte, Claire). [HIGH]
- Governance feat: she "held a domaine with more than 30 shareholders together" (Atkin); today ~40 family shareholders (Brice, Oxford Saïd conversation). [HIGH]

### 5.2 The Olivier split (1994)

Olivier Leflaive, Anne-Claude's cousin, co-managed from 1990 and left in 1994 to concentrate on his négociant house (founded 1984), taking his share of family vineyards — which is why there is an *Olivier Leflaive* Meursault-Blagny Sous le Dos d'Âne as well as the domaine's. A summarised Frederick Wildman page described Olivier's SDA as "a former holding of Domaine Leflaive" — plausible (family partition) but the page is now dead and the wording is unverified. [LOW-MED] Nanson has for years compared "the two Leflaives" and, from 2017, stopped ranking the domaine below Olivier: "differences between these producers are more of style than quality this year." [HIGH]

### 5.3 Pierre Morey (régisseur 1988 – July 2008) — the Perrières neighbour

Domaine Pierre Morey's site: "1988 to the end of July 2008, Pierre Morey becomes the Cellar Master and manager at Domaine LEFLAIVE… taking care of all the technical aspects (vines and cellars) of Domaine Leflaive in addition to his own Domaine and his small négociant-éleveur activity." He went organic/partly biodynamic at his own domaine from 1991 and 100% biodynamic in 1997 — the same year as Leflaive, and his Morey-Blanc négoce (1992) was created to replace the Lafon métayage parcels he lost. His 0.52 ha Meursault Perrières (planted c. 1985, "Perrières dessous… a small, very stony 'clos'… proximity to an old quarry") is the parcel Kelley uses as the landmark for Leflaive's SDA. So the man who made the first white Sous le Dos d'Âne (2002–2007 vintages) farms the vineyard directly below it. Anne Morey, his daughter, is now co-manager. Retired from Leflaive 2008; succeeded by Eric Rémy (BBR). [HIGH]

### 5.4 Eric Rémy and Antoine Lepetit de la Bigne (2008–2016)

Rémy was régisseur/winemaker from Morey's retirement through the 2016 vintage (Nanson tasted the 2015s with him, 20 Oct 2016, when he explained: "From the last (2014) vintage, they have changed everything to DIAM seals here"). Antoine Lepetit de la Bigne was estate manager (Winehog: Pierre Vincent filled "the shoes after Antoine Lepetit la Bigne as estate manager"). The 2006–2013 premox crisis falls in the late-Morey/Rémy years. [HIGH]

### 5.5 Brice de La Morandière (managing partner since 2015)

- Anne-Claude's nephew, Joseph Leflaive's great-grandson. 27 years running multinationals; at the time of her death he was CEO of Hyva (transport equipment), based in Hong Kong and Shanghai. "I knew little about wine, except that I've been drinking the family wine since I was probably 10… I did put my name in the hat, but it came as a surprise, so I was unprepared" (SCMP). He had kept ties — board member, renovated a family house in Puligny. Nanson dates his board role/influence to 2013. [HIGH]
- Decisions: DIAM from 2014 (his first big call — announced to the trade via Corney & Barrow, Oct 2016); hired Pierre Vincent (Jan 2017); cellar renovation; the new cuverie on rue de l'Église (2022) — "It was a question of considering a beautiful setting before asking the question of efficiency"; expansion in the Mâconnais (+7 ha 2016, +13 ha 2020, Pouilly-Fuissé 1er cru Au Vignerais from 2020); Nantoux plantings 2021; the Esprit Leflaive négoce line (from 2018, launched 2022, incl. Pinot Noir and Chablis — Pierre Vincent: "When you love Pinot Noir, you can't live without it."); Club Leflaive. [HIGH]
- Philosophy (Oxford Saïd): "We thrive when we own something that brings something else to life; when we make a living from what we offer rather than what we take." Runs shareholder vine-planting weekends for the ~40 family owners. [HIGH]
- Domaine scale today: 42 ha — 17.24 in the Mâconnais, 9 village/regional, 11.3 premiers crus, 4.74 grands crus (Corney & Barrow). [HIGH]

### 5.6 Pierre Vincent (general manager / winemaker, Jan 2017 – end 2024)

- Grew up in Saône-et-Loire, father a Charolais cattle farmer; viticulture/oenology in Dijon; Antonin Guyon (Savigny), Maison Jaffelin (Boisset), then technical director of Domaine de la Vougeraie 2006–2016 (succeeding Pascal Marchand), where he practised biodynamics. Appointed at Leflaive 6 Jan 2017 aged 42: "Pierre will be in charge of all the day-to-day management of the Domaine's activities in Puligny-Montrachet, in the Mâconnais region and the Leflaive et associés business" and "the rehabilitation of the old cellars" (Brice, via Decanter). [HIGH]
- His first steps (Nanson, 2018): "to tighten the logistics of the wine-making process – arriving at the start of 2017 he could do little more with the wines of the 2016 vintage, but still avoided the movement of unfinished wine between locations." Both he and Brice "like to use the term *precision*". Quote (2017): "80 percent of making wine is the work in the vineyard. It is not the winemakers, it is the land." Biodynamics "seem to give a more precocious maturity" and meant "no acidification was necessary" in 2017. [HIGH]
- Nanson (2018): "Pierre has never openly been a proponent of DIAM" — he inherited the closure decision. [HIGH]
- 2019 was his third vintage; the 2017–2019 trio is where Nanson, Morris ("since 2017, Pierre Vincent and Brice de la Morandière have revitalised the domaine which perhaps needed a more disciplined direction") and Blake ("Together, de la Morandière and Vincent steadied the ship") all date the turnaround. [HIGH]
- Departure: with partners Hervé Kratiroff and Eric Versini (Solexia Group) he bought Domaine des Terres de Velle, Auxey-Duresses (7 ha, 16 appellations incl. Corton-Charlemagne, Meursault Charmes, Puligny Referts, Volnay Ronceret) on 18 July 2023; renamed Domaine Pierre Vincent (announced April 2024); stayed at Leflaive through the 2024 vintage. Philosophy there: "haute couture artisan", 30–70% whole bunch on reds, 10% new oak on whites, six months in tank after barrel "essential". [HIGH]
- Successor: **Amandine Brillanceau**, Technical Director from January 2025 — from Deux-Sèvres, Bordeaux DNO, stints in New Zealand, Australia and Oregon, eight years as cellar master at Louis Jadot (2017–2024); wrote her thesis on biodynamics: "integrating people into nature, which has been here far longer than we have" (Leflaive News #18). Corney & Barrow: she "is already showing promise of pushing the domaine further on its upward trajectory". [HIGH]

### 5.7 Nanson's arc on this exact cuvée (useful table talk)

- 2015 (tasted Oct 2016): "A more agrume, ginger nose – very attractive. Again gas, but here classic Meursault flavours and fine acidity, bigger finishing – just beautiful."
- 2017 (Oct 2018): "the signature clarity and absolute deliciousness that I've seen in many vintages – though less in the 2016 – is here to the fore – it needs more time though."
- 2018 (Oct 2019): "directly a different style of nose; lightly spiced, growing some floral aspect. Full, energetic, dancing over the palate. Absolutely delicious… the nicest of this cuvée for a few years – excellent."
- 2019 (Mar 2021): "Incisive, mineral, energetic – directly a great wine… now this wine is BACK!"
He consistently flags that SDA shows "a different style of nose" from the Pulignys — the Meursault/Blagny signature inside a Puligny range. [HIGH]

### 5.8 Connections to other producers / likely dinner wines

- **Pierre Morey / Morey-Blanc** — Leflaive régisseur 1988–2008; Perrières neighbour; parallel 1997 biodynamic conversion. [HIGH]
- **Domaine des Comtes Lafon** — Morey lost his Lafon métayage parcels (Perrières, Charmes, Montrachet) when Dominique Lafon took them back in the late 1980s/early 1990s, prompting Morey-Blanc; Lafon adopted DIAM from 2013, a year before Leflaive (Goode). [MED-HIGH]
- **Domaine de la Romanée-Conti** — Aubert de Villaine gave Anne-Claude the François Bouchet introduction (1989) and helped found the École du Vin et des Terroirs; both DRC and Leflaive are Wilson Daniels imports in the US. [HIGH]
- **Louis Latour** — the other big name in Sous le Dos d'Âne (Montlivault grapes; "Château de Blagny" Meursault-Blagny; red Blagny SDA with 1/3 new Latour-cooperage oak). [HIGH]
- **Olivier Leflaive** — family; own SDA bottling; K&L: "A Meursault that's almost a Puligny." [HIGH]
- **Domaine de la Vougeraie (Boisset)** — Pierre Vincent's previous 11 years; **Louis Jadot** — Amandine Brillanceau's previous 8 years. [HIGH]
- **Chapelle de Blagny / Martelet de Cherisey** and **Domaine de Blagny / de Bréchard** — the Montlivaut heirs and the only documented Pinot Noir left in SDA (1.00 ha). [HIGH]
- **Benjamin Leroux, Ponsot** — the other high-profile closure dissidents Goode lists alongside Leflaive (screwcap; ArdeaSeal). [HIGH]
