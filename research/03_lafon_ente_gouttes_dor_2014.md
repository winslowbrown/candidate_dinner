# Meursault 1er Cru Goutte d'Or 2014 — Domaine des Comtes Lafon vs. Domaine Arnaud Ente

Dossier prepared for Noah's Chevaliers du Tastevin candidate dinner (Claud, NYC, 26 Oct 2026). Two wines, same climat, same vintage, two of the three or four most important names in Meursault. Expert audience; no basics.

Confidence tags: [HIGH] = primary source or multiple independent sources agree; [MED] = single good source or minor disagreement; [LOW] = single secondary source / inference. "NOT FOUND" means I looked and could not source it.

Two headline corrections to the working assumptions in the brief, up front:

- Lafon's Goutte d'Or parcel is **0.39 ha, replanted 1991**, not ~1.8 ha. Three independent sources agree (Becky Wasserman & Co. tech sheet, Grand Cru Selections importer sheet, Jasper Morris/Inside Burgundy). [HIGH] The ~1.8 ha figure belongs to Lafon's Charmes (1.71–1.90 ha).
- Ente's parcel is **0.22 ha, planted 1990**, and it came to him **through his wife Marie-Odile Thévenot's family**, not from an "Ente family" holding. The Ente family are from the Armentières area in the Nord; Arnaud's grandfather-by-marriage was Camille David of Puligny, and the vines Arnaud farms were the Thévenot family's (father-in-law Philippe Thévenot). [HIGH]

### A2. Name etymology — "the drop of gold" (three competing legends)

1. **The golden wine legend** (the popular story): the name refers to the deep golden colour of the wine. Bouchard's technical director still says "the 'golden' character is immediately visible in the glass." [HIGH that the legend exists; LOW as etymology]
2. **The flint legend** (Louis Latour's version, repeated on every Latour tech sheet since at least 2010): "because of the quantity of flint in the soil. These flints have both the shape and colour of 'drops of gold'." [MED]
3. **The limestone-glint explanation** (Patrick Essa, who has farmed Goutte d'Or for ~25 years at Buisson-Charles): the wine is *not* more golden than its neighbours — "Sa couleur n'est jamais plus dorée qu'ailleurs en dépit de légendes... Au contraire il est la plupart du temps clair comme de l'eau de roche." Instead, "au printemps, en l'absence de végétation, au petit matin lorsque le soleil rasant frappe ce coteau pierreux, les petites dents de calcaire qui le constellent scintillent de mille feux. La pierre aura donc marqué ce climat en déterminant son nom." He adds that the cru is bounded above by a long dry-stone wall broken by rock bars where "le marbre était extrait pour bâtir les maisons et Clos environnants" — so, he says, it could legitimately have been called *Perrières*, "mais en la matière à Meursault le nom était déjà pris!" (Essa, Dec 2021.) [HIGH as his opinion]

Talking point: the one thing all three versions agree on is *stone*. Whichever legend you tell, the punchline that Essa (a Goutte d'Or grower) thinks the wine is usually "clear as rock-water" and *not* golden is the more interesting one for an expert table.

### A3. Soil and geology — the "heavy/deep clay" view vs. the "rocky band" view

The brief's assumption ("heavy/deep soils of the lower-central slope") is *partly* right and the two owners of our wines describe the site differently from each other. Present both:

- **Lafon's view (clay, power):** Grand Cru Selections (Lafon's US importer, information supplied by the domaine): "Clay-rich soil on pebbly limestone sub-soil. Vigorous terroir that must be well-managed. Produces very powerful wines with ripe aromas and explosive minerality." Wasserman: "clay soil with rocky limestone subsoil." Dominique Lafon to Bill Nanson (Oct 2015, tasting the 2014): "More force (it's the clay), more weight." Wine.com's note on the 2017: "the high clay content in the soil provided excellent water retention during the 2017 vintage." [HIGH]
- **Essa's view (thin, rocky, cold, tense):** "Il est marqué par une bande rocheuse en son centre et est de ce fait proche de la roche mère en certains endroits, ainsi les plants ont parfois bien du mal à s'y enraciner et 'donnent toujours naturellement peu' sur ce substrat maigre et argilo-calcaire qui est en fait la résurgence du calcaire dur de Comblanchien sous sa forme équinoderme ici qui s'est abaissée au niveau des Corton pour resurgir ici." He also calls it "sans doute le plus frais... et le plus 'tendu' des crus de la commune, deux jours plus tardifs en moyenne en fin de cycle végétatif," still slightly under the influence of the winds from the Combe d'Auxey, and warns: "Il ne faut surtout pas le couper à trop haute maturité de ce fait car il y perd sa vraie nature." Mikulski: "The subsoil consists of a base of rock plates." [HIGH]
- **Négociant tech sheets:** Latour: "stony marl and Jurassic limestone." Bouchard: "limestone and marl." Gaunoux (T. Edward): "clay soils with a limestone base." [HIGH]
- **Reconciliation:** The cru is a near-perfect rectangle with a rocky band across its middle; the lower part near the water collector and Terres Blanches was re-shaped/filled ("remise en forme"), the middle-south part was re-graded because of a north–south tilt that hindered mechanisation (Essa). So there genuinely is deeper, clay-richer, reworked ground at the bottom and thin rock in the centre — which parcel you own matters. Both Lafon (rows running the full height, per Wasserman's "5–15% slope") and Buisson-Charles ("juste au milieu du climat, les rangs coupent l'ensemble du cru de haut en bas") straddle the band. Where exactly Ente's 0.22 ha sits: NOT FOUND (Kermit Lynch says only "mid-slope"). [MED]
- Contemporary critics' shorthand: Decanter's Meursault profile says of the six central crus "there is no fundamental difference in the terroir" and that the whole band "is set apart by the presence of quarries along the fault lines underground." Burghound (Meadows) on the 2007 Buisson-Charles: "There is absolutely no sense of heaviness present, which Goutte d'Or can sometimes display." [HIGH] That last quote is the critic-consensus stereotype: Goutte d'Or = the richest/most "forceful" of the six, prone to heaviness in hot years.

### A5. History and classification

**18th century.** Courtépée & Béguillet, *Description du duché de Bourgogne* (1778), on Meursault: "Bon vignoble, dont les vins blancs sont renommés dans toute la France. Les meilleurs climats sont les Charmes, les Perrieres, les Gevevrieres, la Goutte d'Or." [HIGH — quoted on fr.wikipedia with citation]

**Thomas Jefferson.** Letter to Étienne Parent (his Beaune agent), 22 January 1789: he asks for "deux cent cinquante bouteilles de vin de Meursault 'Goutte d'Or'" and says he had become so attached to "that of Mr. Bachey of the 1784 vintage that if he still has any of it I would prefer it." Jefferson visited Meursault in March 1787 and ordered Meursault (via Parent) in June and December 1787 as well. [HIGH that the letters exist at founders.archives.gov; the 22 Jan 1789 wording is quoted from a secondary source (Jill Barth, L'Occasion) because founders.archives.gov blocked fetching — MED on exact wording.] Bouchard and winewithseth both use the Jefferson connection in their marketing. This is the best-sourced basis for "the most famous Meursault name of its era": it is the Meursault a foreign connoisseur asked for *by climat name* in the 1780s, at a time when Meursault whites "en sortant du pays, prennent souvent le nom de vins de Mont-Rachet" (Jullien, 1816).

**Jullien, *Topographie de tous les vignobles connus* (1816):** "Le coteau dit la Perrière est particulièrement renommé... Les vignes nommées la Combotte, la Goutte-d'or, le Genevrière et les Charmes, fournissent des vins de la même espèce, et qui, pour le mérite, doivent être classés après ceux de la Perrière, dans l'ordre que j'ai suivi en les nommant." So in 1816: Perrières first, then Combettes(?), Goutte d'Or, Genevrières, Charmes. [HIGH]

**Morelot (1831):** "la petite ville de Meursault devait son antique réputation à ses vins blancs." No Goutte d'Or-specific ranking found. [HIGH for quote]

**Lavalle, *Histoire et statistique de la vigne et des grands vins de la Côte-d'Or* (1855):** for Meursault whites, *tête de cuvée*: Perrières-Dessus and Perrières-Dessous only; *première cuvée*: Genevrières-Dessus, Charmes-Dessus, Bouchères, Tessons, **and la Goutte-d'Or**; *deuxième cuvée*: Porusot-Dessus, Rougeot, Porusot, Grands-Charrons, Chevalières. [HIGH — fr.wikipedia citing Lavalle p.244; winehog's "historic view" article agrees; La Passion du Vin forum transcription of Lavalle's cross-Côte white ranking lists "Charmes, Combettes, Genevrières et Goutte-d'Or à Meursault" in the tier behind Bâtard/Perrières/Corton blanc.] **Caveat:** Bouchard's tech sheet claims Goutte d'Or was "classified 'Tête de cuvée' in 1855 by Dr. Lavalle" — I trust Wikipedia-FR/winehog (both cite the page) over a négociant sheet; Bouchard is wrong or loose. So the correct line for the table is: *Lavalle 1855 — première cuvée, one rung below Perrières.*

**Comité d'agriculture de l'arrondissement de Beaune (1861 classification for the 1862 Exposition universelle):** Meursault had 313 ha of "vignes en vins fins"; **Les Gouttes-d'Or was placed in the 2e classe** (with Terres-Blanches, Petits-Charrons, Rougeots, Tesson, Luraule, Porusot-Dessous...), while Perrières, Charmes-Dessus, Genevrières, Porusot-Dessus and Bouchères were 1re classe. [HIGH — fr.wikipedia citing the 1861 document.] This is the historical "near miss" for Goutte d'Or: in the mid-19th-century official view it was *below* its five neighbours.

**So was it "THE Meursault of the 19th century"?** Not by the classifications. The honest formulation: Goutte d'Or was the *most famous name* (Jefferson, Courtépée), but from Jullien (1816) through Lavalle (1855) and the 1861 Comité, Perrières was always ranked above it and in 1861 it was demoted to second class. Essa's explanation for the name's reputation outrunning its ranking: the name was so attractive that for decades, before the 1er cru rules of the 1950s, Bouchères and other neighbouring parcels were sold as "Goutte d'Or" ("Ainsi Bouchères... a été vendu... en tant que Goutte d'Or pendant plusieurs décennies dans certaines propriétés"), diluting its average quality and identity. He also says it has been "réputée de fort longue date." [HIGH]

**20th century / AOC.** Meursault AOC created 31 July 1937; premiers crus formalised in the 1940s–50s ("nous sommes dans les années 50 pour les 1ers crus" — Essa). Meursault has 19 1er cru climats (incl. Blagny sector) and no grand cru. Why no grand cru: no INAO-era document was found; Decanter's Meursault profile attributes the failure to elevate Perrières to "the fragmented ownership of the vines"; doctorwine calls it "one of the most obvious 'injustices' of the Burgundian classification." The widely-repeated story that Meursault growers in the 1930s preferred the commercial strength of the village name (and lower tax on "village" declarations) is plausible but NOT FOUND in a primary source — say "the received story is..." if you use it. [MED/LOW]

**Modern reputation.** Essa (2012): Goutte d'Or "a gagné de récentes lettres de noblesse depuis que Madame Bize Leroy en vinifie deux petites parcelles" (Domaine d'Auvenay). Coates/Morris-era shorthand: the sixth of the six central crus, often ranked just behind Perrières/Genevrières/Charmes and alongside Porusots/Bouchères.

### 1. The vineyard / climat

### 3. Winemaking for this cuvée (and 2014 specifics)

General white regime at Comtes Lafon (statements 2010–2022):
- **Pressing/settling:** whole-bunch pressed; settled 24 h in tank; "in recent vintages the fermentation has been started in tank before sending the wine to barrel" (Inside Burgundy, updated ~2022). Indigenous yeasts (Wasserman). [HIGH]
- **New oak:** Village wines none; 1ers crus "a proportion that varies from 25% to 40%" (GCS, domaine-supplied); Wasserman "up to 33% new"; Inside Burgundy says "up to 70 per cent for Charmes and Perrières; less for Genevrières and 100 per cent for Le Montrachet, though these are subsequently racked into older wood." For Goutte d'Or specifically: NOT FOUND, but it sits in the 25–40% band — I would say "roughly a third new" and cite GCS/Wasserman. The brief's "~30% new" is consistent. [MED]
- **Cooper/forest/toast:** NOT FOUND for the Lafon whites in any source fetched.
- **Élevage:** Wasserman (Goutte d'Or sheet): "Racked after malolactic. Aged for 18–22 months on fine lees, blended in tank, fining if necessary." GCS: "the wine stays in wood for 16 months." Inside Burgundy: "The wines spend a second winter in barrel, for bottling the following spring or early summer" (Montrachet 18–21 months total). Consensus: two winters in barrel, bottling in spring/early summer of the second year — so the **2014 was bottled roughly spring–summer 2016** [MED, inferred]. Note the domaine cellars are among the deepest/coldest in Burgundy (Cult Wines: "believed to be some of the deepest and coolest in Burgundy"), which is why fermentations and malos are slow and élevage long. [HIGH]
- **Bâtonnage:** "Lees-stirring is used sparingly – 'usually when the fermentation is slow to finish or when the wine is reduced', he says" (GCS). Nanson (2010): after the oxidation problems "instead of batonnage they began rolling the barrels to avoid introducing more air but still getting the fine lees into suspension." [HIGH]
- **Malolactic:** full malo. 2014: "All had quick malos and were often done in Jan/Feb" [2015] — Dominique to Nanson, Oct 2015. Contrast 2013: "Plenty of long and sometimes troublesome malos." [HIGH]
- **Racking:** at Nanson's visit on 23 Oct 2015, "No racking yet for these wines [the 2014s], except the Monthelie." [HIGH]
- **Fining/filtration:** "no fining, reds are unfiltered, whites are filtered if necessary" (Wasserman domaine page) vs. "fining if necessary" (Wasserman Goutte d'Or page) vs. GCS "long aging before being racked and fined." Take: light fining/filtration only as needed. [MED]
- **SO2:** NOT FOUND as a number. René Lafon-era wines were famously low-sulphur/long-élevage; Dominique's answers to premox were physical (see below) rather than a big SO2 increase. [LOW]
- **Closure / premox:** The domaine had documented premature-oxidation problems (Inside Burgundy: "Dominique's approach to white wine making has at times been controversial and the domaine has had its share of premature oxidation, though the issue appears now to be sorted here"). Responses, in order: barrel-rolling instead of bâtonnage; "since 2004 moved to corks that were only coated in paraffin rather than silicone" (Nanson 2010); and **DIAM closures "from the 2013 vintage onwards"** (Inside Burgundy). **Therefore the 2014 Goutte d'Or should be under DIAM** [HIGH on the policy; verify on the bottle — MED that the specific bottling used DIAM]. This is a significant reassurance to give the table on a 12-year-old Lafon white.
- **Style:** Nanson's 2010 profile noted a "style-shift" from the powerful 1990s toward elegance and "minerality that was quite spellbinding." Inside Burgundy: "Dominique Lafon is aiming for purity in his whites." [HIGH]

**2014 specifics for this wine:**
- **Hail:** Nanson's note on the 2014 Goutte d'Or, verbatim: "**70% hail losses.**" (visit 23 Oct 2015). [HIGH] This is the key fact. The 28 June 2014 storm cut across Beaune, Meursault (village-adjacent and northern 1ers crus), Volnay and Pommard; Nanson's vintage summary: "The southern stretches of Meursault lost hardly anything but closer to the village and to its north losses were up to 70%." Goutte d'Or is the 1er cru closest to the village, at the northern end of the band — the worst-placed of Lafon's six. Gilman on the 2014 Bouchères: "the vineyard of Bouchères was right in the path of the hail." Lafon's Charmes (southern end, by Puligny Combettes) was essentially spared. [HIGH]
- **Effect on the wine:** Dominique to Nanson: 2012 (also hailed) "has a bit of strong tannin – probably from the hail – but these 2014s not as the hail was earlier" [i.e., late June, before véraison, so the vines re-set leaves and the surviving fruit ripened cleanly]. Sarah Marsh (WFW): the hail "dramatically reduc[ed] yields without affecting quality since it struck before veraison." [HIGH]
- **Harvest:** Jasper Morris tweeted on 9 Sept 2014: "Lafon starts tomorrow, Ente on thursday" → **Lafon began Wednesday 10 September 2014**; Nanson saw "Lafon and Roulot... in the vines – Meursault Perrières, mainly" that Wednesday afternoon. Goutte d'Or's own picking date: NOT FOUND (Essa says the cru runs ~2 days later than its neighbours; Lafon's Porusots "still one of last to be harvested"). Reasonable inference: 11–16 Sept 2014. [HIGH on domaine start date; LOW on Goutte d'Or day]
- **Production:** at 70% loss on 0.39 ha, the 2014 is a very small bottling — order of magnitude 2–3 barrels / 600–900 bottles vs. a normal ~8–9 barrels. My arithmetic, not a sourced figure. [LOW]

### 4. The vintage as it applied to this wine

**Season (Côte de Beaune, 2014):** mild winter; warm March and early budbreak; cool May with frost scares in Chablis 11–12 May; heat in late May gave a quick, successful flowering; **28 June hail (Saturday afternoon, ~16:30, ~5 minutes)** hitting Beaune (Clos des Mouches up to 90%), Volnay, Pommard (Rugiens 60–80%), Meursault ("at least 40%" overall; up to 70% nearest the village), plus Echézeaux/Clos de Vougeot in the Nuits; then a wet, cool July–mid August; a dry, sunny, north-wind late August/September saved the whites. Harvest in the Côte de Beaune began tentatively ~8–10 Sept, "full commencement" from 13 Sept, mostly finished by 22 Sept (Inside Burgundy). Drosophila suzukii/sour rot was a red-grape problem; the whites were largely clean. Third consecutive year of hail for Volnay/Pommard/Meursault. [HIGH]

**Critics on 2014 whites in general:** Nanson (Nov 2015): "2014 is the greatest white wine vintage that I've ever tasted... If consistency is your only yardstick, 2014 is way out in front... very fine textures, a largely citrus fruit style, and many, many wines deliver their flavour in waves." Jasper Morris: "the most consistent for decades... concentrated, fresh fruit with high acidity." Decanter: Côte d'Or whites 5/5. Sarah Marsh's 2021 retrospective (WFW) was more cautious: "stylish," "haute couture," but most wines faded within 2–3 days of opening, 4 of 14 Côte de Beaune 1ers crus showed oxidative characters early, and for some Meursault "even the premiers crus need drinking up." [HIGH]

**Lafon's 2014s at the domaine:** Nanson, 23 Oct 2015: "Reds and whites were really showing brilliantly. Bravo!" Yields: Volnay Clos des Chênes 2 barrels (normal 6); Porusots "only 16 hl/ha from these 45 year-old vines"; Goutte d'Or 70% loss; Dominique's joke: "Good ripeness but not too much. ### 5. Stories, people, connections

- **Origins.** Jules Lafon — Dijon tax official/lawyer, born in the Lot — married Marie Boch on St Vincent's Day 1894; her dowry included the Clos de la Barre house and cellar (built 1869) and a small négoce. Jules bought Meursault and Volnay parcels and the 0.32 ha of Le Montrachet in 1918/1919 (plot 37, south-east corner, next to DRC's holding). "Comte" was a papal title. First domaine bottling 1935. Sons Pierre and Henri, then René (1927–2019) from 1956, who stopped the family from selling, replanted with the sharecroppers' help, and bottled everything from 1961. Dominique (b. 1958) took over in 1984/85 with brother Bruno (who "has since faded into the background" — Nanson), recovered the métayage contracts and took back Montrachet from sharecropper Pierre Morey in 1991. Handed over formally in December 2021 to daughter Léa and nephew Pierre. [HIGH]
- **La Paulée de Meursault.** Jules Lafon, as mayor, revived/organised the end-of-harvest paulée as a formal banquet (English-Wikipedia: "formally organized in the mid-1920s by the Count Lafon and Jacques Prieur"; most sources say 1923; fr.wikipedia says 1932 — go with "1920s, 1923 by most accounts" [MED]). It is the third of the **Trois Glorieuses** — with the Hospices de Beaune auction and the **Confrérie's Clos de Vougeot dinner** — and Dominique Lafon has helped run it. Direct Tastevin link: the Lafons are the founding family of one leg of the Confrérie-anchored November weekend. Whether Dominique is himself intronised as a Chevalier/Commandeur: NOT FOUND — a question worth asking the table rather than asserting.
- **Biodynamic pioneer.** Herbicides out 1992; organic 1995; 3-year biodynamic trials; whole domaine biodynamic from 1998 — "not very popular in the region, except by a few other pioneers, like Anne-Claude Leflaive and Lalou Bize-Leroy" (GCS). Approach is pragmatic not dogmatic — La Route des Blancs: "pragmatism, sincere humility and capacity to observe." The Charmes is the wine he says improved most under biodynamics (from "very opulent" to "considerably more tension"). [HIGH]
- **Premox and honesty.** Lafon is one of the few top domaines to have openly acknowledged premox and changed practices (rolling barrels, paraffin corks 2004, DIAM from 2013). Good quote for the table (Inside Burgundy): "Dominique's approach to white wine making has at times been controversial." [HIGH]
- **Quotes.** On 2010 Perrières: "My most favourite Perrières – crystal-pure... I don't know if I'll ever make it so good again." On Genevrières: "Not the best wine of Meursault but often the tastiest." On the 2016 whites: "The whites make me think of my 2000s – and I think I made very good 2000s." (all to Nanson.) Daniel Johnnes: "There are few growers anywhere in the world more committed to improving his vineyards... He tirelessly questions his and others' methods." [HIGH]
- **Other hats.** Les Héritiers du Comte Lafon (Mâcon, from 1999); Dominique Lafon négoce label (~4.5 ha, incl. a Puligny 1er Champ-Gains "once worked by Comtes Lafon"), made from 2015 at Château de Bligny with Pierre Murgey; consultant to Evening Land (Oregon) — from Nanson 2015. [HIGH]
- **Levi Dalton, I'll Drink to That:** an episode with Dominique Lafon exists (linked from Becky Wasserman & Co. and Grand Cru Selections pages); episode number/content NOT FETCHED. [MED]
- **Connections to Ente:** same village; both hold Volnay 1er Santenots-du-Milieu (Lafon 3.78 ha, Ente 0.39–0.49 ha) and Puligny Champ-Gains (Ente 0.24 ha; Lafon's négoce); BBR positions Ente "between the generous character of Dominique Lafon and the mineral-driven precision of Coche Dury"; Vinovest: Ente's wines combine "the precision and citrus of Coche-Dury with the richness of the wines by Dominique Lafon." Burgundy Cave sells Lafon as the value alternative "compared to the other top white producers like Coche Dury, Arnaud Ente and Roulot." Both replanted Goutte d'Or 1990/91. [HIGH]

### 3. Winemaking for this cuvée (and 2014 specifics)

Sources disagree on details and the regime changed over time; give the 2014-era picture:

- **Harvest timing:** Ente became "a byword for early picking in a way that has unfairly attracted criticism from others in the village" (Inside Burgundy). Switch from late-picking opulence (1990s) to earlier picking "since 2000" (Morris) / "early-2000s" (LRDB), inspired, he says, by great Alsace Riesling, "comme le Clos Sainte-Hune qu'il cite volontiers." Jasper Morris's tweet of 9 Sept 2014: "**Ente on thursday**" → **Ente began harvest Thursday 11 September 2014**, one day after Lafon and among the first in Meursault. [HIGH]
- **Pressing/settling:** BBR: "White grapes sometimes crushed before pressing; 24-hour must settlement." Sommpicks: "Grapes crushed before gentle pressing; juice settles 24 hours pre-racking." Vinovest: "crushing and hydraulic pressing." LRDB (his own evolution): "les pressurages sont plus doux, les débourbages sont plus serrés" — softer pressing, tighter settling for aromatic precision. iDealwine: "long, gentle pressing." [MED-HIGH]
- **Fermentation vessel / oak:** Kermit Lynch: "20% maximum new oak for all wines"; BBR: "New oak reduced from 35% to 20% for top cuvées"; "Large 600-liter barrels [demi-muids] used for entry-level whites"; Sommpicks: "aging in barrels and demi-muids." Vessel evolution per LRDB: "Des pièces, il passe au demi-muids, plus grands, puis aux foudres, avant d'expérimenter depuis quelques années déjà les globes de verre" (glass "Wine Globes" first used for tiny 2016 lots, bought in size for 2018+; Brasserie du Théâtre 2024: barrels now eliminated entirely). **In 2014 the Goutte d'Or would still have been in pièces/demi-muids with ≤20% new oak** [MED]. Cooper: NOT FOUND.
- **Élevage:** Kermit Lynch: "twelve months in barrel and six months in stainless"; BBR/bestofwines/Sommpicks: "11 months in barrel, then 6 months in tank." → ~17–18 months total; **2014 bottled ~spring 2016** [MED, inferred].
- **Malolactic / bâtonnage:** NOT FOUND (no bâtonnage figure anywhere; assume full malo).
- **Fining/filtration:** "Bottled without fining or filtration" (BBR, bestofwines, Vinovest, Sommpicks). [HIGH]
- **SO2:** NOT FOUND as a number.
- **Closure/premox:** NOT FOUND (no DIAM statement found for Ente). Morris says the 1990s wines were "fleshier but less stable than today." Reductive style noted: Sommpicks — shift toward "a leaner, more focused, and reductive style" using "foudres, glass, and steel vessels"; Brasserie du Théâtre on the Wine Globe: "eliminating oxidation and wood influence while reducing evaporation to a minimum." [MED]
- **Style statement (his own words, LRDB):** "Si la qualité et la vérité du raisin sont là, sur le pied de vigne, mon objectif est d'en perdre le moins possible." And: "L'équilibre, toujours l'équilibre." Kermit Lynch: "Precision, purity, elegance, and finesse are the best descriptors of the Ente style... the baffling paradox of intense old-vine concentration with crystalline weightlessness." [HIGH]
- **Label:** new archer label from 2009 — Morris: "reflects the purity and precision perfectly." The 2014 wears the archer label. [HIGH]

**2014 specifics for this wine:** Ente-specific yield/hail figures for the 2014 Goutte d'Or: NOT FOUND. Given the climat-wide picture (Lafon −70%; "closer to the village... losses were up to 70%"), assume Ente's 0.22 ha was also heavily hailed — plausibly only one barrel or less (a normal year at ~27–30 hl/ha on 0.22 ha is ~6 hl ≈ 2.5 barrels; 2014 likely ≤1 barrel). ### 5. Stories, people, connections

- **Biography.** Arnaud Ente, born 1966; family from the Nord (Armentières); father married the daughter of Puligny vigneron Camille David; brother Benoît was the one expected to take over the family tradition (Benoît Ente has his own Puligny domaine). Arnaud studied biology, failed to get into physiotherapy school, did military service, met Marie-Odile Thévenot, married her in 1991; **in 1990 his father-in-law offered him 3.5 ha of family vines in Meursault; he accepted after two days' thought**, enrolled in a two-year alternating viticulture/oenology course, and took over the vines in 1992 (La Route des Blancs; Inside Burgundy; Vinovest). Address: 12 rue de Mazeray, Meursault. [HIGH]
- **Coche-Dury — verify status:** Retailer/importer sources (BBR: "After working at the legendary Coche Dury"; bestofwines; Sommpicks; Vinovest; wineonp) say he *worked* at Coche-Dury. Jasper Morris's Inside Burgundy overview and Kermit Lynch's sheet do **not** mention it. La Route des Blancs (long French profile with domaine input) says only: "Il n'hésite pas non plus à demander conseil, à ses débuts, à un vigneron qu'il admire, Jean-François Coche, qui n'aura de cesse de l'encourager à suivre son chemin." — i.e., Coche as *mentor/adviser*, not employer. **Verdict: [MED] that he did a stage/period at Coche-Dury; [HIGH] that Jean-François Coche was an early mentor.** Safe phrasing: "mentored by, and by some accounts briefly worked with, Jean-François Coche."
- **The style arc.** 1990s: late-picked, opulent wines that first drew attention (Morris first tasted them mid-1990s on Jean-Philippe Fichet's recommendation); from ~2000: earlier picking, "a more chiselled approach." Morris's two hypotheses — does he like less-ripe grapes, or does his vineyard work ripen them earlier? — resolved in favour of the latter. [HIGH]
- **Bettane & Desseauve "Homme de l'année" (announced Sept 2012, for the 2013 guide):** Michel Bettane: his Chardonnays are "the most accomplished wines in terms of overall balance" in Burgundy and possibly the world; LRDB renders it as "le plus grand vinificateur de chardonnay sur la planète." Arnaud's reaction: "C'est une vraie surprise, je ne pensais pas que le domaine pouvait être hissé à ce rang." Thierry Desseauve: the aim was partly to "showcase vignerons that most enthusiasts don't know." [HIGH]
- **Cult status / secrecy.** Kermit Lynch: "rarely seen outside of his vines or cellar"; the domaine is "totally closed to visits" (Brasserie du Théâtre); French forum members in 2012 debated whether Ente was a "discovery" given that serious collectors had "been discussed for a decade"; demand strongest in France and Japan (vinsdebourgogne.nl). LRDB: "faisant l'objet d'un véritable culte aux quatre coins du monde." [HIGH]
- **Innovation.** Glass jars for the tiny 2016 lots ("because there was not enough volume for even a single barrel of certain vineyards"), then purpose-bought glass globes from 2018; Morris: "Arnaud does not wish them to become the story." [HIGH]
- **Succession.** Son Pierre full-time (reds: 40% whole-cluster, semi-carbonic 3–4 days, no pigeage, stainless élevage — Kermit Lynch 2016 data); son Louis presented wines in London 2018. [HIGH]
- **Connections to Lafon / the dinner:** see Lafon §5. Additionally, Ente's other Meursault parcels (En l'Ormeau, Petits Charrons, Casse-Têtes) are village sites below and behind the village on the same north side as Goutte d'Or; Ente's Puligny Referts abuts Meursault Charmes-Dessous, close to Lafon's Charmes. Ente's Volnay Santenots-du-Milieu (planted 1992) and Lafon's (3.78 ha) are the same climat. [HIGH]
- **Confrérie des Chevaliers du Tastevin / Tastevinage:** nothing found linking Ente to the Confrérie or Tastevinage; he is, by reputation, the anti-institutional figure of Meursault. NOT FOUND.
