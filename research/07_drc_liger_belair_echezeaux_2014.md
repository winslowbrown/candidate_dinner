# Échezeaux Grand Cru 2014 — Domaine de la Romanée-Conti vs. Domaine du Comte Liger-Belair

**Dinner theme: "The Importance of Being Stemmed."** This pair is the theme in a single flight: two Échezeaux from the same commune, the same vintage, harvested nine days apart, from parcels roughly 400 m from each other — one (DRC) fermented with ~70–75 % whole clusters, the other (Liger-Belair) from a "Jayer-iste" house that historically destemmed 100 % and was only just beginning cautious whole-cluster experiments in its crus.

# WINE A — Domaine de la Romanée-Conti, Échezeaux Grand Cru 2014

## 1. The vineyard / climat: Échezeaux

### Location, size, exposure, altitude
- Commune: **Flagey-Échezeaux**, the "plain" village between Vougeot and Vosne-Romanée whose vineyards sit up on the slope and whose two grands crus (Échezeaux and Grands Échezeaux) are the only wines to carry the commune's name — the village-level and premier-cru wines of Flagey are sold as Vosne-Romanée. [HIGH — BIVB]
- Official BIVB figures: **Échezeaux 36.27 ha in production (2022)**, Grands Échezeaux 9.06 ha; 5-year average yield (2017–21) 1,177 hl ≈ 156,541 bottles for Échezeaux. [HIGH — bourgogne-wines.com] Other published totals: 34.79 ha (Wikipedia, 2008 production data), 35.77–37.69 ha (Jefford/Decanter), ~37 ha (Winehog), 38.14 ha (Charles Curtis MW, Decanter 2025, sum of lieux-dits). The spread reflects cadastral vs. planted area. [HIGH that the answer is "36–38 ha"]
- AOC Grand Cru created **31 July 1937** (same decree as Grands Échezeaux). [HIGH — BIVB; Liger-Belair site says "recognized as Grand Cru since July 1937"]
- Exposure east; altitude **230 m to a little over 300 m**, gradient about 13 % at mid-slope (BIVB). Jockovino/Coates-derived description: "faces east from an elevation that reaches 300 meters at the top and 260 meters down below. The slope varies from 10 % at the base to 13 % at the top." [HIGH]
- Geology (BIVB): "The Echezeaux Climats have more diverse soils (largely bajocien marls with pebbly overlay)… Up-slope, the soil is deep (70–80 cm). Gravels, red alluvium, yellowish marl, etc., make up quite a complex mosaic." Jefford (Decanter) and Curtis (Decanter 2022) both emphasise that the vineyard sits at the mouth of the **Combe d'Orveau**, a dry valley through the escarpment whose ancient alluvial fan gives quick-draining soils, gentler slopes, roots farther from bedrock, and a channel for cold air from the Hautes-Côtes — hence a cooler, later-ripening, more "complex and intriguing" site than Vosne's core grands crus. [HIGH]

### Name etymology
- BIVB: the name "derives from chesaux, a word of Gallo-Roman origin meaning a group of dwellings, presumably referring to an ancient hamlet." Jefford: "Echézeaux is derived from casa — cottage or hut in classical Latin." Jockovino/Coates: from Latin *casalis*, a rural house. All agree: "little houses/huts." [HIGH]
- History: first mention in records 1287; acquired by the Abbot of Cîteaux 1353 (Jockovino/Coates). BIVB: like Clos de Vougeot, "founded by the monks of the abbey of Cîteaux and date from the 12th and 13th centuries." The Cistercians farmed this as land outside the Clos de Vougeot wall. [HIGH]

### Classification history and the "dilution" controversy
- Historically, "Échezeaux" proper was the small lieu-dit **Échezeaux du Dessus** (3.55–4.48 ha depending on source). A **1925 court case** (Étienne Camuzet vs. local families including the Gros and Mongeard families — Curtis; Jefford describes it as Camuzet v. Mongeard-Mugneret) established that grapes from the surrounding lieux-dits could also be sold as "Échezeaux"; the **1937 AOC** then swept all of today's lieux-dits into the grand cru. In **1984** a judge added 1.27 ha of Les Beaux Monts Bas on the basis of historical sales records. [HIGH — Curtis, Decanter 13 Jun 2022] Winehog summarises: "expanded from 3.5 ha in 1937" to ~37 ha. [MED on the "3.5 ha" figure]
- Consequence: Échezeaux "has a reputation for being somewhat variable in quality" (Wikipedia) — ~80 owners, most of whom blend across lieux-dits and decline to say where their vines are (Winehog). Steen Öhman (Winehog, 2011/2017) is blunt: "Echézeaux vineyard cannot merit an extraordinary wine; it lacks the complexity and finesse of grand crus like La Romanée, Romanée Conti, Richebourg and Romanée Saint-Vivant" and "A good Echézeaux should at least have some mineral balance from lighter soil, otherwise it's just a big Suchots without freshness and complexity." Charles Curtis's line for the dinner: **"Flagey is doing a lot of the work, and Vosne is getting all the credit."** [HIGH as quotes]

- Quality hierarchy (Winehog, 2011, updated 2017): "The plots placed above Grands Echézeaux and towards Vosne Romanée yield the highest quality. Les Poulaillères, Les Cruots or Vignes Blanches could very well be the premier spots, followed by Les Champs-Traversins, Echézeaux du dessus." Lower tier: Quartiers de Nuits, Rouges du Bas, Beaux Monts Bas, En Orveaux. - Winehog on the two producers at this dinner: "The best Echézeauxs were made by producers Liger-Belair and Henri Jayer, both partly from 'Les Cruots or Vignes Blanches'. Domaine de la Romanée Conti Echézeaux, mainly from 'Les Poulaillères', is also very fine but lacks complexity and intensity." [HIGH as an opinion, dated 2011/2017]

### Neighbours and how Échezeaux differs
- North: Chambolle-Musigny (Musigny/Combe d'Orveau). East: **Grands Échezeaux** and the **Clos de Vougeot wall**. South/west: Vosne-Romanée premiers crus (Les Suchots, Les Beaux Monts, Les Brûlées, Les Rouges). Grands Échezeaux (BIVB): homogeneous, gradient 3–4 %, clay-limestone over Bajocian limestone, 250 m — the calm, structured wine; Échezeaux is the diverse, higher, cooler, gravel-and-marl mosaic that produces (per BIVB) wines of "heightened attack… supple tannins," "rose, violet and fresh cherry" when young, "animal, spice, underbrush, prune" with age. [HIGH]

## 2. DRC's plot within Échezeaux

- **Size: 4.67 ha** (Wikipedia; Wilson Daniels tech page "Surface area: 4.67 hectares"). [HIGH]
- **Location:** overwhelmingly **Les Poulaillères**, the block lying immediately above (west of) Grands Échezeaux in the "heart" of the cru. Corney & Barrow's 2014 release brochure (Adam Brett-Smith, Feb 2017): "the Domaine's Échézeaux has never been in a more beautiful condition with **90 % of the vineyard comprising 'Les Poulaillères'** lying just above Grands Échézeaux." Winehog: DRC ~90 % of Poulaillères (5.21 ha ⇒ ~4.7 ha, which squares with the 4.67 ha total) plus ~10 % of Clos Saint-Denis. [HIGH on Poulaillères dominance; the brief's "Poulaillères, Champs Traversins, Clos St Denis, Cruots" breakdown: Champs Traversins and Cruots were **NOT FOUND** in any source consulted — Winehog lists DRC only in Poulaillères and Clos St-Denis.]
- **Vine age:** Corney & Barrow 2014 vineyard table — **average age 30 years** for Échezeaux in 2014 (vs. 35 GE, 40 RSV, 45 Richebourg/La Tâche, 56 RC); Wikipedia 32 years. This is the youngest red-wine vineyard in the domaine — consistent with the replanting Nanson recorded in 2005 ("The resurgence of this bottling continues") and the "recently replanting part of Echezeaux holdings" noted by Curtis (2022). Precise planting dates NOT FOUND. [HIGH on average age]
- **Ownership structure:** In **1988**, to finance the outright purchase of the Marey-Monge share of Romanée-Saint-Vivant (leased since 1966), "the Domaine de la Romanée-Conti sold their vines in Echézeaux and a smaller amount in Grands Echézeaux, though retained the use of the land on a renewable 30 year lease" (Nanson, 2005 profile); Wikipedia: "financed by the sale and leaseback of the estate's holdings in Échezeaux and some in Grands Échezeaux." So DRC's Échezeaux is farmed under a long lease/leaseback rather than owned outright — a point to verify with the domaine before repeating at table; whether the 2018 renewal changed anything was NOT FOUND. [MED]
- **Plant material:** DRC restocks from its own massal palette: "some 50–60 genetically differentiated vines were identified in Romanée-Conti's 1.8 hectares, and today, the clones of this palette is still used to restock the domaine's vineyards"; three routes — annual replacement of dead vines, uprooting whole sections, and "surgreffage" grafting of in-house selections onto adult vines (Nanson 2005). Bertrand de Villaine (Nov 2014): experiments with 14,000 vines/ha "inconclusive… they would replant at the standard 10K." [HIGH]
- **Farming:** organic through the 1980s–90s; biodynamic trials on ~5–8 ha (La Tâche, Grands Échezeaux) then **fully biodynamic from 2007/2008** ("DRC has been fully biodynamic since 2008 after experiments on 8 hectares proved that this made the wines more expressive" — Bernard Noblet to a visitor, Jan 2010; C&B 2015 brochure refers to copper "permitted under biodynamic regulations"). Horses reintroduced first in Romanée-Conti and Le Montrachet (C&B). Compost from crushed vine roots, grape skins and fermentation residues. Yields "an average of 25 hl/ha… it takes three vines to produce one bottle" (C&B). Chef de culture: **Nicolas Jacob**. [HIGH]

**Confidence range for DRC Échezeaux 2014: 70–75 % whole cluster (HIGH), possibly up to 80 % for this parcel** (the 2013 "70–80 % depending on the parcel" and Nanson's 2014 note that the Échezeaux showed "Whole-cluster flavour… some whole-clusters in the finishing flavour too"). The myth of "100 % whole cluster at DRC" is explicitly corrected by Bertrand de Villaine for the pre-2015 era; 2015 was the exception, not the rule.

Technique detail (Nanson 2015): whole clusters go in **first, at the bottom of the vat, held on a net above the base** to aid drainage; the destemmed fraction is added on top from a destemmer mounted on the vat. This "stems at the bottom" layering is exactly the technique Louis-Michel Liger-Belair described to Craig Williams in 2011 for his own small whole-cluster additions — a nice symmetry for the theme.

### The rest of the regime
- Sorting: grapes "sorted into small baskets and individually examined for health on triage tables" (C&B). In 2014, "Nicolas Jacob and his team effected meticulous selection, in particular eliminating the raisined berries from the heat spike in June and the dried up rot berries of August, as well as the bigger, less ripe grapes" (Brett-Smith, who "ate some of them in the La Tâche vineyard" in Dec 2016). [HIGH]
- Cold pre-fermentation maceration **3–6 days** (Noblet, 2010). Indigenous yeasts (Noblet; general). Fermentation in open wooden vats [MED — widely reported; not confirmed in a source fetched here], length ~3 weeks (2015: 21–23 days). Extraction: by 2018 "a little foulage, more remontage, less pigeage – 'we're looking more for delicacy'" (Bertrand de Villaine to Nanson, Dec 2018) — earlier vintages including 2014 used more pigeage [MED]. Chaptalisation: historically "less chaptalization (none in ripe years)" (Janssen 2013); 2014 NOT FOUND.
- **Oak: 100 % new François Frères barrels** ("All the following wines were tasted from new François Frères barrels" — Nanson, Nov 2014, DRC 2013s; "We always taste from new barrels, but there are some older barrels in the mix too" — Dec 2018). [HIGH on François Frères; MED on strictly 100 % new for Échezeaux in 2014 — the 2018 comment implies a few older barrels exist in the cellar.] The brief's memory that DRC buys its own Tronçais/Fontainebleau wood and dries it before FF coopers it was **NOT VERIFIED** in any source consulted — do not state it as fact. Noblet's line on oak (2010): "un support, comme un soutien-gorge pour une belle poitrine."
- **Élevage: "the standard 16–18 month élevage"** (Nanson Nov 2014); racked once ("The wines have already been racked for the one and only time prior to bottling" — Nanson 2005, of the 2004s). The 2014 brochure: "the wines were put to cask for the long secondary fermentation with final bottling in spring 2016."
- Fining/filtration: in 2018 "we will make a very fine filtration where needed" (Bertrand). 2014 NOT FOUND (assume unfined, light filtration only if needed).
- **2014 Échezeaux specifics (Corney & Barrow vineyard table, 2014 vintage):** average vine age 30; average production 1,340 doz; **2014 production 1,443 doz** (17,316 bottles); **yield 27.8 hl/ha**; **harvest 24, 25, 26 September 2014** — the very last of the domaine's vineyards to be picked; **bottled 25 & 29 February and 3 March 2016**; 13 % abv (Tribeca label data). "Vinifications under Bernard Noblet's serene and stoic eye were naturally easy." [HIGH]
- Chef de cave: **Bernard Noblet** (son of André Noblet, who preceded him) through the 2014 vintage; **Alexandre Bernier** — "with the domaine for 16 years" as of Nov 2025 — succeeded him (Nanson, Nov 2025; exact hand-over date paywalled — the brief's "2018" is plausible but NOT VERIFIED). [MED]

## 4. The 2014 vintage at DRC — and this wine's reviews

### Aubert de Villaine / Adam Brett-Smith's "2014: A Play in Three Acts" (C&B brochure, Feb 2017)
- Epigraph from Aubert: *"Jamais plus qu'en ce millésime 2014 ne se sont vérifiées les deux grandes règles du jeu… la prise de risque, obligatoire, et ce qu'on peut appeler la chance, mais qui est peut-être tout simplement le sourire de la vigne au vigneron qui a su la respecter… et l'aimer !"* ("Never more than in this 2014 vintage have the two great rules of the game revealed themselves… the requirement for risk and what some might call luck, but which is perhaps simply the vines' way of smiling at the winemaker as a sign of gratitude for the respect he showed… and love!")
- Act I: "one of the loveliest – and driest – springs in memory" after "a wet, dark and exceptionally mild winter"; early flowering, "hardly any coulure… but some millerandage."
- Act II: **28 June** — "Spectacular storms erupted along the whole of the Côte, with hail hitting Beaune, Pommard and Volnay with particular savagery and side-swiping Vosne-Romanée," followed by "a canicular spike of heat that literally roasted some of the young berries whose protective leaf canopy had been shredded." July/August "cool, even cold, with very little sun and… over 100 mm of rain in July alone"; rot appeared early August; grass growth between the vines "curiously… had a benevolent influence… as it metered the amount of water available." Nanson's on-the-day report (30 Jun 2014): "hailstones of 2–4 cm diameter in Vosne-Romanée – the Clos de Vougeot and Romanée St.Vivant lost up to 25 %, maybe more in Echézeaux. Much of the rest of Vosne lost more like 15 %." Bertrand de Villaine (Nov 2014): "The hail was not such a great problem in this part of the Côte de Nuits; fortunately it was the grapes that were damaged, not the plants – we lost 15–20 %, which was more like a green harvest for us."
- Act III: "From the beginning of September the blessed North wind arrived… By the end of the second week of September the Pinot Noir was fully ripe and harvesting began on the hill of Corton on the 16th September with La Tâche… on the 17th"; storm on the afternoon of the **19th** (no hail) halted picking; RC picked 19th, Montrachet 22nd, GE 23–24th, **Échezeaux 24–26th**; "Harvesting was completed on the 26th September… dry, warm and bright." Grapes "magnificent, thick skinned, deep coloured and sweet to taste."
- Yields: Corton 25, Échezeaux 27.8, Richebourg 29.75, RSV 31.6, La Tâche 32, GE 32.5, RC 32.6 hl/ha — "After four consecutively short and/or difficult vintages, 2014 marks the intermittent turn of fortune's wheel." Bertrand: "2014 is a more regular volume than 2013 – double!" Total RSV 1,756 doz, Richebourg 1,160, RC 657.
- Aubert de Villaine to Adam Lechmere (Wine-Searcher, Feb 2017): **"2014 is very different to 2015, which had very high phenolics. In 2014, phenolic ripeness came much later. The wines have purity, but they are going to take a long time to reach their full maturity." How long? "About 15 years."** And (via Jukes): "It's a phenolic maturity that gives freshness and a minerality in the sense that it makes the wine a channel for the terroir." Bertrand (Dec 2018): "2017 is a really good vintage that will allow you to drink earlier – 2014 allows that too – we drink quite a few of those now."
- No Cuvée Duvault-Blochet was released in 2014 (Nanson, Dec 2016). Release: Corney & Barrow, February 2017, **£937.50 per 3 bottles in bond** (≈ £312/bt), magnum £635; US importer Wilson Daniels.

The palate is darkly rich, very straightforward, freshly powerful with a grippy structure, a dry mid palate but a good, grunty finish. Heart on sleeve. **Confidence range for Liger-Belair Échezeaux 2014: 0–15 % whole cluster, MEDIUM confidence; best single estimate: fully destemmed or with a token ≤10–15 % of whole bunches laid at the bottom of the vat.** Reasoning: (a) the only cuvée-specific Échezeaux figure ever published is NOT FOUND — every documented percentage concerns Chaumes, Suchots and the experimental Brûlées; (b) Tanzer's report that the 2015 crus got "about 20 %… a bit more than usual" caps the 2014 crus below 20 %; (c) the domaine's Petits Monts is explicitly "never," and Kelley/Cho Lee/Coates all describe a 100 %-destemming house; (d) Nanson's 2014 Échezeaux note (Dec 2015) mentions no stems, whereas he flags them whenever present. **The paradox for the theme:** tasters routinely *perceive* stems in Liger-Belair wines that have none — "Funny that, for ages I always told LM that his wines smell of stems – and he said 'funny, because I don't use them'" (Nanson, 2012); Don Cornutt: "His wines really have a whole cluster type of aromatic profile… Very similar to the aromatics of a Leroy wine." One explanation offered by the domaine's own regime: a full week of cold maceration below 15 °C on uncrushed berries "allows an aromatic extraction, essential to the wines of the Domaine."

### The rest of the regime (domaine website "Winemaking & Ageing," plus Tanzer/Nanson)
- "Louis-Michel Liger-Belair considers that when the grapes are on the sorting table at the entrance to the vat house, **95 % of the work is done**. The remaining 5 % is not a recipe but the application of certain simple principles and a dose of intuition."
- 14 kg perforated crates; sorting table with 8 sorters; destemmed "in whole or in part"; conveyor belts, never pumped; light sulphiting in vat; **cold-soaked below 15 °C for one week**; indigenous fermentation starting "in a few days"; **fermentation ~10 days** (up to 25 days in vat in 2015 — Tanzer); "concluded if necessary with a light chaptalization, divided in two or three days depending on the vintage" (2015: 13–13.5 % "without chaptalization" — Tanzer; 2014: natural 12.4–12.9° per Louis-Michel, so a light chaptalisation in 2014 is plausible — NOT CONFIRMED); "pumping over is carried out, as well as some punching down" — Tanzer 2016: "pumpovers early each morning but only two punchdowns at the beginning of the fermentations – 'and that was it'"; Curtis: punch-downs "greatly reduced" by 2009; Henri Jayer consulted in the 2003 heatwave. [HIGH]
- Small **vertical press**; free-run and press blended; ~10 days settling in tank; barrelled by gravity.
- **Oak: "All the wines are aged in new barrels coming from three barrel-makers and three different forests"** — i.e. **100 % new oak** for every cuvée including village wines ("the whole cellar is new oak here, but some larger barrels, more and more Seguin-Moreau being used" — Nanson Nov 2017; Tanzer 2016: "all of the wines I tasted were in 100 % new barrels, which is now an exception in Burgundy"). Louis-Michel's justification (to Tanzer): "even with one-year-old barrels, my wines are too reduced; there's not enough exchange with air." Nanson 2004: 100 % new oak "because Louis-Michel prefers long aging without racking… he needs the cleanest fruit and barrels to avoid any taint." Coopers other than Seguin-Moreau NOT FOUND (2004: "two different barrels; both from the same maker, the wood coming from different forests"). [HIGH on 100 % new; MED on coopers]
- **No racking during élevage; no SO2 addition until the racking before bottling**; malo before or after the first winter; racked by gas pressure (not pumped) into tank **10–18 months after harvest**; sulphited; "2 to 3 moons" in tank under inert gas; **no fining, no filtration; gravity bottling**; corks oriented one by one; shipped in wooden cases with straw and silk paper. [HIGH]
- **2014 specifics:** Louis-Michel to Nanson (Dec 2015): "We began our harvest on **September 15** with serious phenolic ripeness – **12.4–12.9°** and there was super deep colour right from the beginning. The first part of aging was difficult, **the malos being long and late**." Domaine yields "30 hl/ha in 2014 (roughly)" vs 22 (2013), 25 (2012), 28 (2011). The 2014 wines were sulphured "first time, the day before racking" (village Vosne, Dec 2015); the 2014 La Romanée "was bottled 2 months later" than usual (Nanson, Nov 2016). Exact Échezeaux 2014 bottling date NOT FOUND (the pattern implies spring–summer 2016). Nine days earlier than DRC's Échezeaux pick (24–26 Sept). [HIGH]

## Talking points for the flight ("The Importance of Being Stemmed")
1. **Same vintage, opposite doctrines.** DRC 2014: ~70–75 % whole cluster, stems netted at the bottom of the vat, picked 24–26 Sept, 27.8 hl/ha, 16–18 months in new François Frères, Noblet's last decade. Liger-Belair 2014: Jayer-school destemming (≤15 % stems at most, probably none in the Échezeaux), week-long cold soak under 15 °C, two punch-downs, 100 % new oak from three coopers, no racking, no SO2 until bottling, picked from 15 Sept at 12.4–12.9°.
2. **The Jayer/DRC Échezeaux argument, re-run.** Jayer's Cruots, destemmed and chaptalised, vs. DRC's Poulaillères, whole-cluster and late-picked (Janssen 1990 comparison). Liger-Belair holds 0.33 ha of Cruots and learned at Jayer's knee.
3. **Perception vs. fact.** Critics (Nanson, Cornutt, Carnes, Kelley's interlocutors) "smell stems" in Liger-Belair wines that have none — a good blind-tasting trap for the committee.
4. **Bertrand de Villaine's myth-buster:** "Most people assume that the domaine only uses whole cluster fermentations, but normally there's about 25–30 % destemming" (2015). 2015 was the first ~100 % whole-cluster year "for many years."
5. **Terroir hierarchy inside Échezeaux:** Poulaillères and Cruots are the two lieux-dits most often named as the best (Winehog; Curtis; Jayer) — these two bottles are, arguably, the two best addresses in the cru.
6. **Tastevin hooks:** Aubert de Villaine and the 2015 UNESCO Climats inscription; the Liger-Belairs' 8 ha in the Clos de Vougeot (1889) and Louis-Michel's return to the Clos in 2015.
